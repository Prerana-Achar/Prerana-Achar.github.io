      A GOOD PLACE TO BEGIN
      A little guidance.
      A lot of playing.
      1. Select a song. Choose from the library or import a PDF or MIDI from files. MIDI opens directly. For a PDF, use an up-to-date Chrome, Edge or Firefox. The recognition engine loads automatically and detects notes on your device. Clear printed sheets work best. Review the result for recognition mistakes.
      2. Make it yours. Choose your instrument, a comfortable speed, a starting measure, and how many measures to practice. Set the measure count to 0 for all remaining measures.
      3. Start with the notes. Leave Check timing off to play at your own pace. The score waits on each note until you get it right. Wrong notes turn red, then green when corrected. Turn Check timing on for rhythm practice. The clock waits until you play the first correct note, then the phrase follows the beat.
      4. Revisit a phrase. Pause, review the red notes, and try again.
      Use a quiet room and one note at a time. Avoid playing the reference audio through speakers while practicing. Enable Automatic playback beside Follow the score to hear the expected note after a mistake. Let the sound fade and pause briefly before trying again; playback itself won’t count as your answer. Repeated notes need a fresh attack; a held note won’t count twice. Microphone and room latency can affect timing—adjust the delay correction when needed.
      PDF recognition: homr (AGPL-3.0), with a browser MIDI converter. PDFs are processed in your browser with the Sheetscan port of homr. Models download on first use; no launcher or Python installation is needed.
      MIDI is performance data, not an engraved score. Displayed rhythm is rounded to sixteenths; grading uses the original MIDI timing, including tempo changes. Your instrument sets the sound for both Hear this part and Automatic playback, as well as the displayed clef and written pitch. Listening is checked at concert pitch. Instrument samples load from this site; if a sample cannot be read, a synthesized fallback plays.
      Instrument audio: FluidR3 GM samples by Frank Wen, rendered by Benjamin Gleitzman (CC BY 3.0).
